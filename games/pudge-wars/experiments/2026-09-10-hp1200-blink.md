# Base policy after 1,200 HP and fountain crossings

The native 3v3 Base update beat its frozen predecessor **19–5** on fresh,
bank-paired seeds. Against local `myasnik_v75.bas`, it improved from **22–2
to 24–0**, with mean opposing kills falling from **18.42 to 13.21**.
These are local version-36 simulations, not hosted leaderboard results.

## What changed

- Native team fishing uses stable player lanes, counting dead teammates
  and excluding gadgets. One nearby, living, unhooked teammate on its bank
  approaches the next rune; the others keep their distance.
- In team games, falling below 40% HP starts recovery that continues to
  75%. Urgent combat, dodges and rescue handling retain their priorities.
  The Python policy keeps its existing deliberate allied healing and spacing.
- A stranded teammate can Blink home through the fountain pinch when the
  entire landing is within 20 tiles and the visible path is clear. Bodies,
  walls, Rot and wide-river jumps prevent that crossing.
- Team-game dodges head inland on the bank actually occupied. Using team
  affiliation for that direction could send a caught player into the lava.
- Native duel behavior is retained: the team candidate increased timeouts
  in 1v1. Its previous formation includes the effect of friendly wards on
  lane counting; removing that behavior also regressed the duel check.
  The selected native policy reproduced all 24 previous duel game hashes.
  Python likewise retains the 40% retreat trigger for duels, with its safer
  Blink logic still enabled. Its extra duel check was 11 wins and one
  timeout versus the control's 12 wins; do not claim a duel improvement.

The JSON host now advertises `game_version`. The Python policy requires
version 35 or later for a bank crossing; older hosts keep their original
cross-bank restriction. No physics or reward changes were made in this study.

## What 1,200 HP means for strategy

An unmodified 200-damage hook needs six hits from full health, versus five
at 840 HP. Focused follow-up damage and avoiding repeated deaths become more
valuable than assuming a single catch will finish a target.

Percentage healing scales up: the neutral fountain gives 18 HP/second
instead of 12; the home pad gives 48 HP/second instead of about 32. The
120-point allied hook heal is now 10% of starting HP rather than 14.3%.
Recovering from 40% to 75% restores about 420 HP at the new starting health.
That is just under nine seconds on the home pad, excluding travel and combat.

The extra health buys survival time, but does not make every Blink invasion
profitable. The 20-tile hop can clear the narrow center and cannot clear the
24-tile side-lane gap. Rot still blocks Blink. Ordinary center camping and
guarded offensive crossings underperformed the existing ranged formation.
Keep that formation and use the new crossing to escape when it is available.

## Experiments and selection

All development used seeds 6101–6106 (Python component screens used
6101–6104). Final native confirmation used 6201–6212 on both banks.
Games had normal fog, fair native difficulty, a 20-minute cap, and goals of
30 kills for 3v3 or 10 for 1v1. Native fair difficulty schedules a bot every
eight ticks. JSON policies decide each tick; replies are applied on the next
tick through the actual host adapter. Compare within an adapter, not across
the two tables. Network latency and hosted infrastructure are not simulated.

Native development, 12 games per row against Myasnik:

| Change | Goal wins | Mean score |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Frozen base | 10 | 28.67–22.08 |
| Recovery to 75% alone | 11 | 29.33–20.92 |
| Retreat at 30% instead | 10 | 29.25–20.42 |
| Three damage ranks after three range ranks | 9 | 27.83–22.42 |
| Center fountain fishing | 9 | 28.92–22.67 |
| Center fishing plus guarded raids | 9 | 28.92–21.75 |
| Stable lanes and one collector | 11 | 29.75–18.75 |
| Lanes plus recovery | 12 | 30.00–17.75 |
| Lanes, recovery and safer escapes | 12 | 30.00–16.50 |

The recovery-only native head-to-head was 6–6; spacing-only was 10–2.
The combined result supports the package, not a claim that recovery alone
reliably improves win rate. Keep range → speed → damage → radius upgrades.

Fresh native checks, 24 games per row:

| Matchup | Wins–losses–timeouts | Mean score |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Previous base vs Myasnik, 3v3 | 22–2–0 | 29.50–18.42 |
| Selected base vs Myasnik, 3v3 | 24–0–0 | 30.00–13.21 |
| Selected base vs previous base, 3v3 | 19–5–0 | 28.88–21.58 |
| Previous and selected base vs Myasnik, 1v1 | 23–1–0 each | 9.83–5.25 each |

The original all-mode candidate scored 20–0–4 in the duel holdout. A further
6301–6312 duel check of shortened recovery still regressed. The selected
policy therefore retains the prior native duel decisions. A full 48-game
equivalence rerun reproduced both the accepted 3v3 candidate and the previous
duel state hashes; it is verification, not additional independent evidence.

Fresh Python checks:

| Matchup | Games | Wins–losses–timeouts | Mean score |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Recovery and escapes vs previous Python base, 3v3 | 16 | 9–7–0 | 26.38–24.13 |
| Recovery and escapes vs Myasnik, 3v3 | 16 | 16–0–0 | 30.00–16.13 |
| Selected Python vs Myasnik, 1v1, seeds 6301–6306 | 12 | 11–0–1 | 9.83–4.33 |
| Previous Python on the same duel seeds | 12 | 12–0–0 | 10.00–4.08 |

The Python improvement remains directional. Its center-camping/raiding screen
lost 3–5 against the previous Python policy; that behavior was not retained.
Timeouts above mean the kill goal was not reached, even when a policy led on
score. Hosted scoring may resolve such leads differently.

## Evidence and reproduction

[Machine-readable results](2026-09-10-hp1200-blink-results.json) preserve every
completed screen, confirmation, failed candidate and equivalence check.
Local artifacts are under `duels/hp1200-policy-study/`: frozen policies,
source and runner hashes, request settings, results, per-game logs and replays.
The JSON instrument imports the real observation/action adapter; it mixes
Python policies with frozen BASIC opponents and records acceptance echoes.
No league credits were used for these simulations.

All 72 recorded native holdout games verified every replay tick. Exact replay
inspection of the 24 selected-vs-Myasnik team games found **one bank-to-bank
escape and zero land-to-water Blink landings**. The crossing was player 6 on
seed 6202, east side, at tick 11115 (5:47.34), from (58.95, 64.01) to
(71.5, 64.5), with 532 HP. Python had two accepted escapes in its Myasnik
holdout, including one with 212 HP. These examples establish that the new
branch runs; they do not isolate its contribution to win rate.

Example repeat of the primary native comparison on new seeds:

```sh
python3 scripts/eval_pudge_policy.py \
  examples/pudge_wars/players/base.bas \
  duels/hp1200-policy-study/myasnik_v75.bas \
  duels/new-base-comparison \
  --runner duels/hp1200-policy-study/pwheadless \
  --first 6401 --last 6412 --team-size 3 --goal 30 --record
```

The 14 native combat/formation checks, 48 Python policy/analysis checks,
native bot, economy, teammate, JSON protocol and JSON recording suites passed.
They cover continued recovery, kill-earned HP, death reset, both crossing
directions, unsafe landings, bank-aware dodges, stable lanes and rune roles.
